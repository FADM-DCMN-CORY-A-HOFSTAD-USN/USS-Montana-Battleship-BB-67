1\. The Stack Manifold (Top-Deck Dual Intake)
---------------------------------------------

The traditional midship smokestacks of the *Iowa* and *Montana* classes are redesigned as a hybrid multi-medium fluid intake. Because the engine faces two completely hostile extremes (high-pressure ocean water and zero-pressure space vacuum), the stacks are divided internally:

-   Water Submersion Mode (The Hydro-Jet Intake):

    -   When the *Montana* dips below the surface, the top of the stacks open wide.
    -   Massive amounts of ocean water flood directly down into a waterproof, isolated central channel.
    -   A Siemens MV Motor (like the real-world massive industrial marine motors) drives a heavy multi-stage mechanical impeller inside this channel, sucking water from the stacks and violently blasting it out of the rear sealed hydro-jet nozzle to generate immense forward momentum like a giant armored jet ski.

-   Space Flight Mode (The Cosmic Gas Recirculator):

    -   When the ship leaves the atmosphere, the open water channels are locked down tightly by heavy mechanical blast doors to prevent the ship's internal atmosphere from venting into space.
    -   A secondary set of internal valves opens, routing compressed Xenon or SF₆ propellant gas *from internal storage tanks* directly into the electroacoustic wave drivers. The stacks effectively become closed-loop balancing vents for internal pressure.

Waterproofing the Space Thruster
--------------------------------

Saltwater is highly corrosive and conducts electricity, meaning it would instantly short-circuit the Snap Circuit high-voltage bus and ruin the acoustic sound-wave drivers.

-   The Solution: When submerged, heavy titanium sliders completely seal off the *Electroacoustic Propulsion Core* from the main intake pipelines. The core remains filled with dry, pressurized nitrogen gas to keep ocean moisture away from the high-voltage rectification plates and electrets.

Spaceproofing the Water Jet
---------------------------

In the vacuum of space, any residual liquid water left inside the Siemens MV hydro-jet plumbing would instantly boil off or freeze solid, cracking the internal steel housings.

-   The Solution: Before the *Montana* uses its electroplate array to lift off into space, the Siemens MV motor runs a brief high-velocity air purge cycle. This blasts all remaining seawater out of the intake and exhaust lines. Once dry, vacuum-tight gaskets seal the water pump chamber completely to prevent internal freezing.

* * * * *

3\. The Power Grid: High-Voltage Snap Circuits
----------------------------------------------

Because the system switches between the mechanical Siemens MV motor (demanding massive current to fight water resistance) and the electrostatic plates/acoustic thrusters (demanding ultra-high voltages to fight gravity), the internal power grid needs to be incredibly adaptive:

-   Sub-Surface Transits: The high-voltage snap circuit buses are configured to route power straight from the ship's auxiliary generators down into the Siemens MV motor step-down transformers to maximize torque.
-   Atmospheric Takeoff: The snap circuit mechanical bridges "snap" into a new sequence, completely disconnecting the marine motors and rerouting the main energy pipeline into the custom rectifier array, dumping total negative electron saturation across the 360 hull segments to achieve lift.
